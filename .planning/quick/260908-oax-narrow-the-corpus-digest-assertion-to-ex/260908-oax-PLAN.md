<objective>
Narrow the corpus-digest assertion so `mkv_opus_a.webm` and `mkv_opus_b.webm` are excluded from
byte-exact comparison only -- not from the corpus, not from `gen_corpus.sh`, and not from any test.

Purpose: WINDOWS.md #22 records that the designated leg fails its own digest assertion
non-deterministically. Real CI run 34021508083 (head `dfc9e8d`) passed the step; run 34022461121
(head `6b57c2a`, a docs-only diff -- no source changed) failed it on the same leg, with the byte
divergence confined to exactly those 2 of 80 fixtures. `ffmpeg -h encoder=libopus` reports
`Threading capabilities: none`, and running both recipes 5 times each on one fixed host produced
1 distinct SHA-256 per recipe -- so the jitter is strictly cross-host: libopus does its own runtime
CPU-feature dispatch over float DSP, and `-flags +bitexact` is an ffmpeg-level flag that does not
reach inside a third-party encoder. The `ubuntu-latest` label is not a fixed physical machine.
This is the same class as WINDOWS.md #12, manifesting within one architecture-labeled leg.

**This is not fixable as a bug.** No change can make libopus emit identical bytes across
heterogeneous CPUs. The assertion is currently claiming a property those two files do not have,
and a gate that fails on docs-only commits is on its way to being ignored -- which is the
project's own stated P0 failure mode applied to its own CI.

Output: a self-testing `scripts/assert_corpus_digest.sh` that compares 78 of the 81 digest lines
with a named, counted exclusion; ci.yml wired to it; the given-up coverage documented; WINDOWS.md
#22 resolved honestly.
</objective>

<design_decisions>
**Where the exclusion lives: a new `scripts/assert_corpus_digest.sh`.** Three sites were
considered.

- *Inside `scripts/corpus_digest.sh`* -- rejected. That script is invoked by a separate,
  always-on "Report corpus digest" step on **every** leg (ci.yml ~174-180, T-3-82), and its
  `CORPUS_DIGEST_SUMMARY=` line plus its 80 per-fixture lines are the raw material for measuring
  cross-platform byte-identity from run-log evidence rather than assuming it. Filtering there
  would delete the only place the two Opus hashes are ever printed -- destroying evidence on all
  5 legs in order to fix an assertion that runs on 1 -- and would silently change the meaning of
  the summary line that 03-19's cross-leg analysis rests on.
- *Inline in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`* -- rejected. Inline workflow bash cannot be executed or
  self-tested locally, and this change's whole risk is that an exclusion accidentally disables the
  entire comparison, which looks identical to success from the outside. Proving the guard fires
  and the gate is non-vacuous requires runnable code.
- *A new script invoked by that step* -- chosen. `corpus_digest.sh` stays byte-unmodified, the
  evidence stays complete on every leg, and the gate becomes locally runnable and self-testing,
  matching the established convention in `check_corpus.sh` and `lint_bash4_builtins.sh` where a
  gate proves it can still fire before it reports clean.

**Three lines are excluded, not two.** `CORPUS_DIGEST_SUMMARY=` is a SHA-256 *of* the full 80-line
listing, including the two Opus lines. It cannot be byte-stable while they are not. Excluding it
costs zero coverage, because every line it summarizes is compared individually. Leaving it in
would have made the entire change inert.

**`tests/golden/CORPUS_DIGEST.txt` is not edited.** It stays the complete, faithful 81-line record
of what the designated leg's corpus produced (80 fixtures + summary). Consistency with the
assertion is maintained by the assertion naming, on every green run, exactly which of those lines
it did not compare -- rather than by mutating the record into something that no longer matches
what `corpus_digest.sh` prints.

**Coverage deliberately given up.** After this change, `mkv_opus_a.webm` and `mkv_opus_b.webm`
have **no byte-level drift detection on any leg**. A real, silent change to either file's bytes --
a gen_corpus.sh recipe edit, an ffmpeg pin bump that alters Opus output -- would not fail CI. What
still covers them asserts structure and findings, not bytes:
`tests/integration/test_container_mkv.cpp:119,140` (compare/inspect behavior),
`tests/unit/test_ebml_scan.cpp:383` (EBML element offsets, PROBE-05),
`tests/integration/test_doc03_coverage.cpp:135` (coverage bookkeeping). This is why arm64-osx
passes all of them today while holding byte-different fixtures. This change is not free and is not
presented as free.

**Explicitly out of scope:** changing the audio codec in `gen_corpus.sh` (the fixtures exist to
exercise Opus-specific Matroska features -- `CodecDelay`/`SeekPreRoll` -- with `mkv_noopus.mkv` as
their deliberate counterpart; swapping the codec destroys their purpose); forcing libopus
determinism via ffmpeg flags (established impossible above); touching `EXCLUDED_TEST_REGEX` or
`EXPECTED_EXCLUDED_COUNT` in the Test step, which govern WINDOWS.md #17's byte-exact *test*
exclusion and are unrelated to the digest listing.
</design_decisions>

<deferred_ideas>
- **Structural digest for the Opus fixtures.** Hash the *parsed EBML structure* (element IDs,
  sizes, ordering, CodecDelay/SeekPreRoll values) instead of file bytes. That would restore drift
  detection for these two fixtures without depending on encoder byte-stability. Recorded as the
  named alternative on the new WINDOWS.md entry; not scope here.
</deferred_ideas>
